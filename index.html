Schedule I

The Business, in Plain Terms

ICICI Prudential Life sells life insurance, pensions, annuities and health riders to Indian households. It earns money two ways: an underwriting margin (premium collected minus claims paid and costs) and investment income on the ~₹31 lakh crore of policyholder and shareholder funds (its "float") that it manages until claims fall due. It commenced operations in FY2001 and today ranks among India's top private life insurers by sum assured.

Why this matters for a value investor

  • Insurance is a trust business — the vision statement's emphasis on "sensitivity" lines up with a 25-year, zero-default investment record and a 99.3% claim settlement ratio.
  • Bancassurance (ICICI Bank branches) is the largest distribution leg — a durable, low-cost customer acquisition channel most standalone insurers don't have.
  • Product mix is shifting toward protection and annuity — segments IRDAI flags as structurally under-penetrated in India ("Insurance for All by 2047").

Cash Flow — Where the Money Actually Moved

FY2026 vs FY2025 cash flow (₹ Crore)

Standalone Receipts & Payments Account

Reading an insurer's cash flow (don't panic at the negative)

  • Operating activities were cash-negative in both years (−₹5,341 Cr FY26, −₹9,400 Cr FY25). For an insurer this is normal, not a distress signal — premium collected is classified as operating cash-in, but the moment that cash is deployed into government bonds and corporate debt to back policyholder liabilities, it shows up as an investing outflow instead.
  • Investing activities were strongly cash-positive (+₹7,557 Cr FY26), driven by net proceeds from churning the ₹2.9+ lakh crore investment book and interest/dividend income of ≈₹11,973 Cr — a good proxy for the recurring "float income" of the business.
  • Financing activities were roughly neutral to slightly negative in FY26 (−₹171 Cr) after the company repaid ₹1,200 Cr of older subordinated debt and re-raised ₹1,196 Cr of fresh sub-debt at 7.69% — a routine capital-structure refinancing, not distress borrowing.
  • Net result: cash & equivalents rose from ₹7,878 Cr to ₹9,924 Cr over the year — the balance sheet is building, not burning, cash.

Growth, Measured as a Value Investor Would

Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) smooths out year-to-year noise to show the true underlying growth rate over the FY2022→FY2026 period (4 years).

PAT CAGR — 20.7%

₹754 Cr → ₹1,600 Cr. Profit is compounding faster than premium (12.2% CAGR), meaning the business is getting more efficient, not just bigger — the single most important trend on this page.

EPS CAGR — 20.5%

₹5.25 → ₹11.06. Tracks PAT closely, meaning growth hasn't come from diluting shareholders with excess new share issuance.

Net Worth CAGR — 10.4%

₹9,163 Cr → ₹13,631 Cr. Slower than PAT growth because the company pays out a growing dividend rather than retaining every rupee — reasonable for a mature, cash-generative insurer.

Embedded Value CAGR — 13.8%

₹316 Bn → ₹530 Bn. EV is the insurance industry's version of intrinsic book value (net worth + present value of future profit on in-force policies) — this is the number long-term insurance investors watch most closely.

VNB CAGR — 5.0%

₹21.6 Bn → ₹26.3 Bn, with a dip in FY2024. VNB is "profit on business signed this year" — it is lumpier because it moves with product mix and cost ratios, not a smooth compounder like PAT.

AUM CAGR — 6.9%

₹2.40 Trn → ₹3.14 Trn. Slower AUM growth than PAT growth is actually healthy here — it shows profit growth is coming from margin discipline, not simply from a bigger pile of assets.

Schedule V

Pros & Cons — and Why They Matter

✓ Strengths

Profit growth is accelerating, not just continuing
FY2026 PAT grew 34.6% YoY versus a 5-year CAGR of 20.7%. When the latest year beats the trend, it usually means recent cost-optimisation and mix-shift efforts are compounding — worth confirming this continues in FY2027 results before assuming it's structural.
Fortress balance sheet, zero NPA since inception
Solvency ratio of 227.3% is well above the regulatory minimum of 150%, and 94.5% of the fixed-income book sits in sovereign or AAA paper. For an insurer, this is the single best proxy for "will they be able to pay claims and survive a bad cycle" — the core of what value investors mean by margin of safety.
Distribution moat via ICICI Bank
Bancassurance is the largest distribution channel (~30% of APE). Bank-branch access is a durable, low customer-acquisition-cost channel that pure agency-led insurers cannot easily replicate.
Best-in-class claims experience
Individual claim settlement ratio has climbed from 97.8% (FY2022) to 99.3% (FY2026), with a 1.1-day average turnaround. This directly protects the brand — the product being sold is, ultimately, a promise.
Rising, sustainable dividend
Dividend per share has grown roughly 3x (₹0.55 → ₹1.65) over five years while the payout ratio stayed conservative (dividend well under 20% of PAT), leaving capital for growth and solvency buffers.

✗ Weaknesses / Risks

VNB — the truest "new business quality" metric — has been lumpy
VNB fell from ₹27.65 Bn (FY23) to ₹22.27 Bn (FY24) before recovering, and its 5-year CAGR of just 5% badly lags PAT growth. This means profit growth has partly come from the back-book (old policies) and cost control rather than purely writing more profitable new business.
A meaningful slice of "income" is market-driven, not core underwriting
Net investment income swung from ₹9,910 Cr (FY23) to ₹46,598 Cr (FY24) to ₹10,881 Cr (FY26) — largely mark-to-market gains/losses passed through to policyholders. A new investor should look past headline "total income" swings and focus on PAT, VNB and EV instead.
Ownership overhang from Prudential Corp's stake sale
In May 2026, Prudential Corporation Holdings (UK) proposed divesting its 21.89% stake, pending regulatory approval. Large stake sales can pressure the stock price in the near term and raise questions about the long-term shareholding structure, even though ICICI Bank remains the majority owner.
Not statistically "cheap" on absolute earnings multiples
Trailing P/E is ≈46x and P/B is ≈5.4x — high by general-market value-investing yardsticks. Insurers are properly valued on Price/Embedded Value instead (currently ≈1.4x, discussed in Schedule VI), but a newcomer should not screen this stock using a plain P/E filter.
Regulatory and tax changes move margins directly
The September 2025 GST reform reduced input-tax-credit availability and pushed up effective costs even as it helped affordability for customers; cost-to-premium held flat at 18.2% only because of offsetting efficiency gains. Insurance margins are structurally exposed to rule changes outside management's control.
Schedule VI

What Price Makes Sense for a Long-Term, Buy-and-Hold Investor?

Life insurers are not valued on plain P/E — earnings are lumpy and partly non-cash. The industry standard is Price / Embedded Value (P/EV): EV already captures net worth plus the discounted value of profit locked into existing policies, so P/EV tells you how much you're paying for growth beyond that.

Where today's price sits on the P/EV spectrum

Today ≈ ₹510 (1.4x EV)
< 1.3x EV (≈ <₹475) 1.3x – 1.8x EV (≈ ₹475–₹660) > 2x EV (≈ >₹730, seen at Oct 2024 all-time high of ₹795)
Deep value zone Fair value zone Richly valued zone

The arithmetic

  • FY2026 Embedded Value: ₹529.89 Bn ÷ ≈144.9 Cr shares ≈ ₹366 EV/share
  • At ₹510/share, that's ≈1.39x EV — below the ~2x–2.9x multiple the market paid at the FY2024–25 highs (₹680–₹795), but above the sub-1x levels seen in broad market sell-offs.
  • The stock is down ≈28% from its ₹795 all-time high (Oct 2024) to its 52-week low of ₹459.50 (Apr 2025) — the current price is roughly midway through that range.

A simple, disciplined approach for a first-time value investor

  • Buy zone: Historically, entries at or below ~1.3x–1.5x EV (roughly ₹475–₹550 today) have offered a reasonable margin of safety relative to the company's own 5-year valuation history.
  • Watch, don't chase, above ~1.8x–2x EV (≈₹660–₹730) — that band has coincided with subsequent multi-quarter corrections in this stock.
  • Because you plan to hold long-term, consider buying in tranches (SIP-style) across a few quarters rather than timing a single entry — this reduces the risk of buying right before a short-term dip.
  • Re-check EV and VNB margin every year at annual-report time (usually April–June) — rising VNB margin with stable-to-rising EV growth is the health check that matters most for this business.

This is an educational valuation framework built from the company's own disclosed EV and market price, not investment advice — Claude is not a SEBI-registered advisor. Please do your own due diligence or consult a licensed financial advisor before investing.

Tested Against Peter Lynch's Rules

Lynch (One Up on Wall Street, Beating the Street) favoured understandable businesses, reasonable growth-adjusted valuations, and clean balance sheets. Here's how ICICI Prudential Life stacks up.

1Invest in what you understandStrongLife insurance is conceptually simple: collect premium, invest it, pay claims. This dashboard's glossary aside, the business model needs no specialist expertise to follow.
2Categorise the stock (Lynch's six types)StalwartNot a "fast grower" (VNB CAGR only ~5%) nor a slow grower (PAT CAGR 20.7%) — best described as a Lynch "stalwart": a large, steady, moderately-fast compounder you hold for years, not a multi-bagger you flip.
3PEG ratio (P/E ÷ growth rate) reasonable?PartialP/E ≈46x against a 5-yr EPS CAGR of ~20.5% gives a PEG of ≈2.2 — above Lynch's "PEG near 1 is fair" rule of thumb, though PEG is a poor fit for insurers since P/E itself is a weak metric here (see Schedule VI).
4Strong balance sheet, manageable debtStrong227.3% solvency ratio (min. required 150%), zero NPA since inception, and the only debt on the book is a modest, freshly-termed ₹1,196 Cr subordinated debenture used to reinforce solvency — not to fund operations.
5Company has a defensible niche / moatStrongExclusive-feeling bancassurance access through ICICI Bank plus 25 years of brand trust and claims track record — a moat that's difficult and slow for a new entrant to replicate.
6Avoid "diworsification" into unrelated businessesStrongThe company stayed focused on its core life-insurance license; it even divested its pension-fund-management subsidiary to ICICI Bank in FY2026 rather than expanding into unrelated lines.
7Insider buying / management confidenceNot disclosed hereAnnual reports show ESOP allotments (management being granted shares) but not open-market insider purchases — that data sits in exchange filings, not the annual report, so it isn't assessed on this page.
8Steady or growing dividend (a sign of confidence)StrongDPS has risen every year bar one flat year, from ₹0.55 to ₹1.65 over five years — a 3x increase, while the payout ratio stayed conservative, leaving room for further increases.
9"Turn over the rocks" — do the unglamorous homeworkStrongThe company's own disclosures (cost ratios, persistency, solvency, VNB walk) are granular enough that a patient retail investor can actually verify claims year-on-year — an unusually transparent insurer by Indian standards.
10Avoid the "hot stock in a hot industry" trapPartialInsurance is not a hyped sector today, which Lynch would like — but the stock did trade up to a euphoric ~2.9x EV multiple at its Oct-2024 high before correcting, showing it isn't immune to sentiment swings.
Lynch-style verdict: This fits Lynch's "stalwart" bucket well — understandable business, real moat, clean balance sheet, rising dividend. The one rule it doesn't cleanly satisfy is valuation-vs-growth (PEG): at today's price the stock isn't the bargain-relative-to-growth-rate Lynch preferred, even though it's cheaper than its own recent history.

Glossary — Terms Used on This Page

Embedded Value (EV)Net worth of the company plus the discounted present value of profit expected from policies already sold. The insurance industry's version of "intrinsic book value."
Value of New Business (VNB)The profit (in present-value terms) the company expects to earn from all the new policies it sold this year alone.
VNB MarginVNB ÷ Annualised Premium Equivalent (APE). Shows how profitable each new rupee of business written actually is.
APE (Annualised Premium Equivalent)A standard way insurers add up premium from different policy types (single-pay, regular-pay) on a comparable annual basis.
Assets Under Management (AUM)Total investments the company manages on behalf of policyholders and shareholders — its investment "float."
Solvency RatioAvailable capital ÷ capital required by the regulator (IRDAI) to safely cover claims. Minimum required is 150%; higher is safer.
Persistency RatioThe percentage of policyholders who keep paying premiums and don't lapse their policy — a proxy for customer satisfaction and product quality.
Claim Settlement RatioPercentage of death claims the insurer approved and paid out of all claims received — the ultimate test of whether the company honours its promise.
BancassuranceSelling insurance policies through a bank's branch network — here, mainly ICICI Bank.
ULIP / Linked BusinessA policy where part of the premium is invested in market-linked funds chosen by the customer, who bears the investment risk/reward.
Non-Linked / Participating BusinessTraditional policies with guaranteed or bonus-linked returns; the insurer, not the customer, manages the underlying investment risk.
Net WorthShareholders' equity — paid-up capital plus reserves; the accounting "book value" of the company.
P/E, P/B, P/EV RatiosPrice divided by Earnings, Book Value, or Embedded Value per share respectively — how many rupees you pay today for one rupee of that metric.
PEG RatioP/E ratio divided by the earnings growth rate. A rough Peter Lynch yardstick for whether a growth stock's price is reasonable relative to how fast it's growing.
CAGRCompound Annual Growth Rate — the smoothed, year-over-year growth rate that gets you from a starting value to an ending value over a number of years.
Operating / Investing / Financing Cash FlowThe three buckets of the cash flow statement: cash from core business operations, cash used to buy/sell investments and assets, and cash from raising or repaying capital/debt.
MoatA durable competitive advantage (brand, distribution, cost, network) that protects a company's profits from competitors — a term popularised by Warren Buffett.
Stalwart (Peter Lynch term)A large, steady company growing earnings at a moderate, dependable pace — not explosive, not stagnant — typically held for years rather than traded.
